Your example curriculum

24 courses · 180 EC · Starting in autumn 2026

Year 1

Semester 1

  • Personal & Professional Development

    ACCPPDE101 · 7.5 EC

    This course is a first-year requirement and aims to stimulate personal development, namely, being able to actively, consciously and ethically orientate oneself in relation to others and the world. It supports the development of core personal and academic skills that enhance critical thinking and independent learning. It seeks to improve and develop academic writing and presenting skills and helps students develop the foundational skills and attitudes to reflect on societal issues and engage with societal partners.

  • Introduction to Western Art History

    MCCHEAM111 · 7.5 EC

    Introduction to Western Art History surveys Western art from late-medieval and Renaissance painting to contemporary practice. Students relate artists, styles and movements to social, religious, political and cultural change. Close examination of paintings develops visual analysis, while a term paper combines study of a painting on location with art-historical evidence.

  • Introduction to Literary Studies

    MCCLITR111 · 7.5 EC

    Introduction to Literary Studies introduces practical criticism and literary interpretation. Students examine what literature is, why it is read and studied, formal properties and the constructed character of texts. Fiction, poetry and drama are supplemented by contemporary nonfiction and basic film narration, developing critical terms, tools and techniques.

  • Introduction to Film & Media

    MCCMEDI111 · 7.5 EC

    Introduction to Film & Media examines how moving images create meaning through cinematography, editing, sound, narrative and performance. Film history connects early cinema and major movements with feminist and postcolonial interpretation, alongside television, digital platforms and spectatorship. An analytical or creative-analytical project combines close analysis, theory and scholarly research.

Semester 2

  • Great Literary Works

    MCCLITR112 · 7.5 EC

    Provides a chronological overview of literature from the epic of Gilgamesh to post‑modernity. Reads literary and critical texts that explore human experience and teaches how to approach literature’s ambiguities and trace themes across ages.

  • Style & Meaning

    MCCCOMM211 · 7.5 EC

    Style & Meaning studies stylistics: how linguistic choices produce meaning and interpretive effects, principally in literary texts and also in nonfiction. Students connect grammar, discourse and cognition with interpretation through frameworks for dialogue, narration, modality, deixis and text worlds. Analysis exercises lead to an original research-supported stylistic analysis.

  • Qualitative Research Methodology

    GOSSOCI212 · 7.5 EC

    Introduces qualitative research methodology. Covers designing questions, interpreting meaning and conducting small team projects, while reflecting on complexity and power dynamics in social research.

  • Creative Communication Laboratory

    MCCCOMM312 · 7.5 EC

    Creative Communication Laboratory combines close reading and creative workshops with poetry, rhythm, sound, voice and performance. Students develop traditional or hybrid creative projects grounded in scholarship and aesthetic traditions. Revision and reflection require them to explain their formal, conceptual and procedural choices.

Year 2

Semester 1

  • Modern History

    GOSHIST111 · 7.5 EC

    Provides a chronological overview of history from 1815 to the present. Discusses industrialisation, popular sovereignty and national self‑determination, and covers major economic, political and cultural developments including revolutions, world wars and the fall of communism.

  • Media Archaeology

    MCCMEDI112 · 7.5 EC

    Explores media technologies before digital culture. Studies cassette tapes, typewriters and early printing as material objects that carry meaning; includes hands‑on experiments; no technical background required.

  • World Myth & Literature

    MCCLITR211 · 7.5 EC

    World Myth & Literature compares mythic narratives across ancient and later traditions, examining transmission, cultural connections and themes such as creation, power, gender, love and loyalty. Greek, Roman, Near Eastern, Indian, Arabic and African materials support literary analysis. A research paper, graphic explanation and creative retelling connect interpretation with narrative practice.

  • Modern Sociology

    GOSSOCI214 · 7.5 EC

    Examines the trajectory of sociology within the culture of imperialism and colonial responses. Studies leading social theories and debates about modernity and critically engages with sociological discourses.

Semester 2

  • Life & Travel Writing

    MCCLITR212 · 7.5 EC

    Life & Travel Writing examines memoir, autobiography, diaries, personal essays, travel writing and digital self-presentation. Students explore memory, identity, autobiographical truth, ethics and cultural positioning through close reading and theory. A personal writing portfolio and an independent life-narrative project connect analysis with practice.

  • From T.V. to TikTok

    MCCMEDI211 · 7.5 EC

    From T.V. to TikTok examines American social history and everyday life from the mid-twentieth century through contemporary media. Television, film and digital forms connect storytelling and power with identity and imagined futures. Students compare dominant portrayals with Indigenous self-representation, investigate disability representation, and develop futures-oriented creative projects.

  • Communicating the Climate Crisis

    MCCCOMM212 · 7.5 EC

    Examines how the climate crisis is communicated and contested. Analyzes texts, films, exhibitions, protest art, slogans and social media alongside theory and case studies, teaching students to dissect arguments and culminating in a project where students enter the debate.

  • African Art & Heritage

    MCCHEAM211 · 7.5 EC

    Examines the artistic, historical and ethical dimensions of African art. Highlights distinct forms and cultural significance, considers influence on Western modernism and addresses looting, colonial-era acquisitions and repatriation efforts.

Year 3

Semester 1

  • Modern Art & Modern Museums

    MCCHEAM312 · 7.5 EC

    Examines debates on modern art through various themes. Focuses on the relationship between art and its presentation in museums and exhibitions, exploring curatorial practices and exhibition strategies.

  • Media & Race

    MCCMEDI311 · 7.5 EC

    Media & Race investigates storytelling, power, privilege, resistance and systemic racism in American history from the late nineteenth century to the present. Film, photographs, music and other media connect past and present. Students produce research-based podcasts exploring how media sustains oppression and supports resistance.

  • Film & Text

    MCCLITR311 · 7.5 EC

    Film & Text studies relationships between literary and film narratives, adaptation and intertextuality. Students learn methods of film analysis and examine plot, character, setting, image, acting, music and sound, alongside the relationships among adaptors, critics and viewers.

  • New Issues in Contemporary Social Theory

    GOSSOCI311 · 7.5 EC

    New Issues in Contemporary Social Theory studies civil society through critical, neo-functionalist and decolonial perspectives. Topics include trust, social capital, public spheres, solidarity, social movements and civil repair. An experience report connects engagement or observation to course concepts, while an individual project uses theoretical and empirical sources to analyse a civil-society organisation, movement or initiative.

Semester 2

  • Writing Art's Histories

    MCCHEAM316 · 7.5 EC

    This course examines the history of art history as an academic discipline, tracing how scholars have understood and interpreted art from the sixteenth century to the present. Through critical readings of influential texts by figures such as Aby Warburg, Erwin Panofsky, Ernst Gombrich, Michel Foucault, and Jacques Derrida, students explore the major ideas and methodologies that have shaped the field. Topics include art and history, aesthetics, meaning and interpretation, authorship and identity, and the challenges of art history in a globalized world.

  • Perspectives on Literary Meaning

    MCCLITR312 · 7.5 EC

    Examines how different perspectives—humanist, feminist, Marxist, cultural minority and psychoanalytic—affect reading and interpretation. Students undertake a research project applying selected perspectives to texts.

  • Media & the Environment

    MCCMEDI212 · 7.5 EC

    Examines representation of environmental concerns through visual culture. Explores the Anthropocene, animal representation, deep time, biocentrism, contested nature, indigenous and postcolonial media; analyzes how media shape environmental understanding; and includes a paper or media project.

  • The Documentary

    MCCMEDI312 · 7.5 EC

    Introduces documentary film from its early development to contemporary forms, analysing historical and social contexts, styles and genres, and ethical and aesthetic questions. Students analyse non-fiction representations and produce a short documentary item.
