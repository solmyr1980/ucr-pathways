Your example curriculum

24 courses · 180 EC · Starting in autumn 2026

Year 1

Semester 1

  • Personal & Professional Development

    ACCPPDE101 · 7.5 EC

    This course is a first-year requirement and aims to stimulate personal development, namely, being able to actively, consciously and ethically orientate oneself in relation to others and the world. It supports the development of core personal and academic skills that enhance critical thinking and independent learning. It seeks to improve and develop academic writing and presenting skills and helps students develop the foundational skills and attitudes to reflect on societal issues and engage with societal partners.

  • Foundations of Comparative Politics

    GOSPOLI112 · 7.5 EC

    Provides analytical tools to compare political systems and democracies. Examines phenomena like democratic backsliding, decline of political parties, rise of populism, media changes, non‑majoritarian institutions and globalization’s impact on domestic politics.

  • Introduction to Sociology

    GOSSOCI111 · 7.5 EC

    Introduces concepts, theories and methods of sociology. Examines socio‑historical context of key authors like Marx, Weber, Durkheim and Du Bois, and encourages exploration of social problems related to race, gender and class through theoretical approaches and methodologies.

  • Introduction to Economics

    BENECON111 · 7.5 EC

    Explore how individuals, firms, and societies make economic decisions. This course introduces key concepts from both microeconomics and macroeconomics, including markets, incentives, and the principles of supply and demand, and provides a foundation for analyzing real-world economic issues.

Semester 2

  • Research Methods & Statistics

    BENGATE102 · 7.5 EC

    Research Methods & Statistics introduces research design, operationalisation, measurement, sampling, surveys and experiments. Students study descriptive statistics, estimation, hypothesis testing and introductory regression using R and R Commander. A group project develops a research plan and instruments, collects and analyses data, and reports findings under research-ethics and privacy requirements.

  • Theory of International Relations

    GOSPOLI212 · 7.5 EC

    Theory of International Relations introduces competing approaches to international relations and geopolitics, including realism, liberalism, Marxism, constructivism and critical theory. Students apply theories to historical and contemporary cases. A United Nations Security Council simulation develops country-position research, negotiation and collaborative resolution drafting.

  • Qualitative Research Methodology

    GOSSOCI212 · 7.5 EC

    Introduces qualitative research methodology. Covers designing questions, interpreting meaning and conducting small team projects, while reflecting on complexity and power dynamics in social research.

  • Ethics

    GOSPHIL212 · 7.5 EC

    Ethics examines moral reasoning through historical and contemporary theories and close philosophical reading. Students study utilitarianism, Kantian ethics, virtue ethics, contractarianism and feminist ethics, alongside Nietzsche, Murdoch and Nagel. Reflection and research essays develop interpretation, argument evaluation and an independent position on ethical theory or a practical ethical problem.

Year 2

Semester 1

  • Law, Society & Justice

    GOSGATE102 · 7.5 EC

    Law, Society & Justice examines legal, social and normative perspectives on law. Students compare legal traditions and study public and private law, human rights, justice, social control and change, race and gender. Case briefs, demand letters and mock trials develop legal reasoning, while a family-law topic supports the design of a socio-legal research proposal.

  • Human Rights: Law & Philosophy

    GOSLAWJ111 · 7.5 EC

    Offers a legal, historical and philosophical approach to human rights. Studies classical and modern philosophers, traces the rise of human rights, examines the US Bill of Rights and the French Declaration, and discusses human rights treaties.

  • International Macroeconomics

    BENECON211 · 7.5 EC

    Study the economy as a whole through topics such as national income, economic growth, inflation, and unemployment. Understand how governments and central banks use fiscal and monetary policies to influence economic performance over time. The course also explores business cycles, international trade, and economic crises, preparing students to evaluate policy debates and global economic trends.

  • Modern Sociology

    GOSSOCI214 · 7.5 EC

    Examines the trajectory of sociology within the culture of imperialism and colonial responses. Studies leading social theories and debates about modernity and critically engages with sociological discourses.

Semester 2

  • Introduction to Public International Law

    GOSLAWJ212 · 7.5 EC

    Introduction to Public International Law examines its sources, theories, institutions and relationship to domestic law. Topics include responsibility, force, armed conflict, international crime, human rights, economic relations and environmental protection, followed by self-determination, Third World approaches, compliance and treaty politics. Case exercises and a research podcast develop legal reasoning and critical analysis.

  • Comparative Constitutional Law

    GOSLAWJ213 · 7.5 EC

    Comparative Constitutional Law compares constitutional structures, institutions and rights across legal systems and introduces administrative law. Students study federalism, legislatures, executive relationships, courts, judicial review, human rights, public participation and procedural fairness. A staged booklet compares two constitutions, supported by legal interpretation, case-law analysis and hypothetical cases.

  • Public Economics

    BENECON311 · 7.5 EC

    Investigate the role of government in the economy, focusing on how public policy influences efficiency and equity. The course covers taxation, public goods, externalities, and government spending. Combining theoretical models with empirical evidence, it evaluates how different policies affect societal welfare.

  • Foundations of European Union Law

    GOSLAWJ312 · 7.5 EC

    Introduces the EU legal order. Studies institutions, framework, citizenship and the internal market using problem‑based exercises and guest lectures.

Year 3

Semester 1

  • New Issues in Contemporary Social Theory

    GOSSOCI311 · 7.5 EC

    New Issues in Contemporary Social Theory studies civil society through critical, neo-functionalist and decolonial perspectives. Topics include trust, social capital, public spheres, solidarity, social movements and civil repair. An experience report connects engagement or observation to course concepts, while an individual project uses theoretical and empirical sources to analyse a civil-society organisation, movement or initiative.

  • Introduction to Political Philosophy

    GOSPOLI111 · 7.5 EC

    Introduction to Political Philosophy examines political thought from classical antiquity to contemporary debates, including European and non-European thinkers. Students study views of human nature, society, rights and authority in historical context and compare their arguments with current political problems. Original texts, an essay and a group project develop interpretation and normative argument.

  • Psychology of Organizations & Leadership

    BENLEAD311 · 7.5 EC

    Psychology of Organizations & Leadership examines the psychological, social and structural dynamics of organisations. Case discussions and research-based frameworks address motivation, leadership, group dynamics, organisational culture, power, conflict and change.

  • European Union Politics

    GOSPOLI311 · 7.5 EC

    European Union Politics examines EU institutions, actors, integration and policymaking, including representation, legitimacy and decision-making. An empirical project investigates European Parliament committee amendments, relating MEP ideology and possible interest-group influence to amendment activity. Students plan the research, code amendments, assess salience and trace legislative processes.

Semester 2

  • Public Policy Analysis

    GOSPOLI312 · 7.5 EC

    Investigates public policy in the context of globalization. Uses a case study on sugar to examine policy‑making phases and alternative conceptualisations across national and international arenas. Students write policy memos.

  • Econometrics

    BENECON312 · 7.5 EC

    Econometrics supplies tools to analyze data to answer questions of causality and prediction. In this course you learn three estimators—Ordinary Least Squares, Generalized Least Squares, and Instrumental Variables—and three tests—t-test, F-test, and LM-test. Understanding the basic rules of econometrics, particularly the sampling distribution concept, is key, and the course uses Monte Carlo simulations in Stata to visualize it. Tools are applied to estimate economic relationships and make predictions, and students write an empirical paper.

  • Microeconomics & Behavior

    BENECON212 · 7.5 EC

    Study how consumers and producers make decisions under scarcity. Explore key concepts such as rational choice, decision-making under uncertainty, and non-egoistic preferences. Analyze market structures, strategic interactions, and factor markets. Learn how markets function and examine the impact of regulation. Develop analytical tools to better understand behavior and engage with empirical research.

  • Psychology of Leading Under Pressure

    BENLEAD211 · 7.5 EC

    Examines how emotion regulation, emotional intelligence, belonging, identity safety and psychological safety shape leadership in diverse contexts, with particular attention to minority experiences. Students design and pilot a quantitative survey, collect data ethically, and use SPSS or RStudio for descriptive statistics, reliability analysis, correlations, group comparisons and introductory multiple regression. They interpret the findings and develop evidence-based leadership recommendations.
