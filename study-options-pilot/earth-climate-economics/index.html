Your example curriculum

24 courses · 180 EC · Starting in autumn 2026

Year 1

Semester 1

  • Personal & Professional Development

    ACCPPDE101 · 7.5 EC

    This course is a first-year requirement and aims to stimulate personal development, namely, being able to actively, consciously and ethically orientate oneself in relation to others and the world. It supports the development of core personal and academic skills that enhance critical thinking and independent learning. It seeks to improve and develop academic writing and presenting skills and helps students develop the foundational skills and attitudes to reflect on societal issues and engage with societal partners.

  • Sustainability Essentials I

    ENSGATE101 · 7.5 EC

    Introduces sustainability through planetary boundaries and scientific tools for understanding human impacts on Earth systems. Students interpret graphs and uncertainty, build simple dynamic models, and study chemical and physical principles relevant to climate, energy and materials. A laboratory project makes and tests starch-based biopolymers.

  • Introduction to Economics

    BENECON111 · 7.5 EC

    Explore how individuals, firms, and societies make economic decisions. This course introduces key concepts from both microeconomics and macroeconomics, including markets, incentives, and the principles of supply and demand, and provides a foundation for analyzing real-world economic issues.

  • Introduction to Data Science

    DISDATA111 · 7.5 EC

    Develops foundational skills in data visualization, cleaning and manipulation. Explores spatial and text data and includes hands‑on work with datasets from various disciplines.

Semester 2

  • Sustainability Essentials II

    ENSGATE201 · 7.5 EC

    Develops physical-science foundations for sustainability through waves, chemistry and materials science. Reflective work and a project apply these topics to analysing greenwashing.

  • Research Methods & Statistics

    BENGATE102 · 7.5 EC

    Research Methods & Statistics introduces research design, operationalisation, measurement, sampling, surveys and experiments. Students study descriptive statistics, estimation, hypothesis testing and introductory regression using R and R Commander. A group project develops a research plan and instruments, collects and analyses data, and reports findings under research-ethics and privacy requirements.

  • Natural Hazards, Risks & Adaptations

    ENSENVI112 · 7.5 EC

    Covers geoscience principles and analytical tools to understand natural disasters. Compares risks from climate change‑driven events and mining disasters and teaches actions to reduce risks from events like earthquakes, droughts and toxic algae blooms.

  • Mathematical Methods

    DISGATE102 · 7.5 EC

    Mathematical Methods develops foundational techniques in differentiation, integration, complex numbers and differential equations. Students solve problems analytically and with Mathematica. A project implements, analyses and adapts a dynamic model, requiring interpretation of its behaviour and computational results.

Year 2

Semester 1

  • Dynamic Earth

    ENSENVI111 · 7.5 EC

    Introduces Earth Science by focusing on interactions among the deep earth, lithosphere and hydrosphere. Highlights key cycles shaping Earth’s surface and includes indoor/outdoor labs and a multi‑day field excursion.

  • Climate Change

    ENSSUST111 · 7.5 EC

    Focuses on drivers and impacts of anthropogenic climate change on sea level, ocean acidification, ecology and society. Discusses adaptation and mitigation solutions, including renewable energy systems, carbon storage and climate‑adaptive planning, and examines climate governance and justice.

  • Building with Nature

    ENSDELT111 · 7.5 EC

    Introduces the sustainable engineering approach of working with natural processes for flood protection, land reclamation and port development. Covers history and principles of ecosystem‑based design, explores case studies and includes guest lectures and field excursions.

  • Introduction to Biodiversity

    ENSECOL111 · 7.5 EC

    Introduces the diversity of living organisms, from bacteria and archaea to protists, plants, fungi and animals. Students examine evolutionary relationships, distinguishing characteristics and ecological roles. Practical work introduces microscopy, organism identification and biodiversity measurement, supported by a small project on a selected branch of the tree of life.

Semester 2

  • Earth Observation

    ENSENVI211 · 7.5 EC

    Provides hands‑on experience with GIS and data integration using geospatial data from satellites and drones. Uncovers how human actions impact ecosystems and climate and promotes sustainable practices through research projects.

  • Soils & Aquifers

    ENSENVI212 · 7.5 EC

    Explores soils and aquifers through fieldwork and lab analyses. Covers soil formation, properties, health and remediation and investigates mapping aquifers for geothermal energy, coastal defence and freshwater resource assessment.

  • Microeconomics & Behavior

    BENECON212 · 7.5 EC

    Study how consumers and producers make decisions under scarcity. Explore key concepts such as rational choice, decision-making under uncertainty, and non-egoistic preferences. Analyze market structures, strategic interactions, and factor markets. Learn how markets function and examine the impact of regulation. Develop analytical tools to better understand behavior and engage with empirical research.

  • Spatial Planning

    ENSSUST315 · 7.5 EC

    Explores spatial engineering and geospatial technologies like GIS and remote sensing for addressing global challenges such as natural disasters, food shortages, resource decline and pollution. Emphasises hands‑on projects in disaster response, sustainable agriculture and environmental monitoring.

Year 3

Semester 1

  • Environmental Biogeochemistry

    ENSENVI311 · 7.5 EC

    Examines chemical and biological controls on interactions among water, minerals, rocks and soils. Topics include equilibrium, speciation, redox chemistry, adsorption, weathering and biomineralisation. Laboratory work, geochemical modelling and an environmental project connect these processes to contaminant behaviour, wastewater treatment, remediation and resource recovery.

  • R3: Recycling, Recovery, Remediation

    ENSDELT311 · 7.5 EC

    Examines strategies and technologies towards a circular economy. Explores resource security and stewardship, develops lab skills and inorganic chemistry knowledge, and connects to themes at the Delta Climate Center.

  • Probability & Statistics

    DISMATH314 · 7.5 EC

    Provides a foundation in mathematical statistics. Covers probability theory, discrete and continuous distributions, sampling, parameter estimation and simulation; uses computer algebra software to develop theoretical understanding and practical skills.

  • Energy & Sustainability

    ENSSUST211 · 7.5 EC

    Introduces renewable energy resources and technologies, discussing environmental, political and economic constraints. Covers energy storage and demand management and aims to balance energy supply and demand.

Semester 2

  • Living with Water

    ENSDELT211 · 7.5 EC

    Studies water risks and opportunities in deltas, using Zeeland as a central case. Flooding, freshwater scarcity, salinisation, subsidence and pollution are connected to infrastructure, history, governance, law and justice. Students evaluate engineered and nature-based responses, then apply these ideas through site-based inquiry and a project on delta adaptation.

  • Earth's Resources

    ENSENVI312 · 7.5 EC

    Provides in‑depth geoscience research into resource and environmental challenges such as nuclear waste storage, geothermal energy and CO₂ capture. Students conduct literature reviews and produce class reports for presentation to a professional conference.

  • Governance of Sustainability

    ENSDELT312 · 7.5 EC

    This course examines sustainability through the lens of governance: “a process of rule-making and collective decision-making that includes both governmental and non-governmental actors in shaping public policy” (Fenger & Bekkers, 2016). Taking this lens, we ask questions such as how are sustainability policies on the local, national, and international level made and enforced? Which actors represent which interests, and which are most influential? How is public opinion on sustainability shaped, and how does public opinion shape politics? What, at the end of the day, determines effective policy for sustainability? By the end of this course, you will have gained a deep appreciation of the complexity of the governance dimension of sustainability, and have developed the theoretical and practical understanding to critically assess mainstream policy decisions.

  • Econometrics

    BENECON312 · 7.5 EC

    Econometrics supplies tools to analyze data to answer questions of causality and prediction. In this course you learn three estimators—Ordinary Least Squares, Generalized Least Squares, and Instrumental Variables—and three tests—t-test, F-test, and LM-test. Understanding the basic rules of econometrics, particularly the sampling distribution concept, is key, and the course uses Monte Carlo simulations in Stata to visualize it. Tools are applied to estimate economic relationships and make predictions, and students write an empirical paper.
