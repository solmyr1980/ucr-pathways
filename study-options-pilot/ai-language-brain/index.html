Your example curriculum

24 courses · 180 EC · Starting in autumn 2026

Year 1

Semester 1

  • Introduction to Programming

    DISGATE101 · 7.5 EC

    Introduces programming and computer science. Covers structured programming, data constructs, testing, debugging and object‑oriented programming with Python, and develops computational problem‑solving skills.

  • Introduction to Brain & Behavior

    HCBGATE102 · 7.5 EC

    Explores why people behave as they do and how brain injuries affect mental processes. Discusses nature and nurture, introduces brain anatomy and psychological theories, and provides a gateway to cognitive science and clinical psychology.

  • Statistics & Experimental Methods

    HCBPSYC211 · 7.5 EC

    Develops experimental-design knowledge and practical statistical analysis for health and behavioural research. Students choose, fit, diagnose and interpret multivariable models for group comparisons, categorical outcomes and latent constructs. R, primarily through R Commander, supports practical exercises; data-analysis papers and a poster connect research questions to appropriate models, assumption checks and clear reporting.

  • Personal & Professional Development

    ACCPPDE101 · 7.5 EC

    This course is a first-year requirement and aims to stimulate personal development, namely, being able to actively, consciously and ethically orientate oneself in relation to others and the world. It supports the development of core personal and academic skills that enhance critical thinking and independent learning. It seeks to improve and develop academic writing and presenting skills and helps students develop the foundational skills and attitudes to reflect on societal issues and engage with societal partners.

Semester 2

  • Mathematical Methods

    DISGATE102 · 7.5 EC

    Mathematical Methods develops foundational techniques in differentiation, integration, complex numbers and differential equations. Students solve problems analytically and with Mathematica. A project implements, analyses and adapts a dynamic model, requiring interpretation of its behaviour and computational results.

  • AI Ethics

    DISARIN111 · 7.5 EC

    AI Ethics examines artificial intelligence from philosophical and psychological perspectives. Topics include fairness, privacy, responsibility, human-value alignment, moral status and political or existential risks. Students apply ethical frameworks to questions about human behaviour and interaction, synthesise research in an analytical psychology project, and produce a research-based podcast.

  • Neurobiology

    HCBCOGN112 · 7.5 EC

    Neurobiology studies nervous systems and behaviour through comparative anatomy, evolution and physiology. Students examine animal behaviour, research models, welfare and research ethics, then connect biological rhythms and regulatory behaviour to sleep, lifestyle drugs, mood and eating disorders. Comparative-anatomy work and a literature-based report apply neurobiological concepts.

  • Psycholinguistics

    HCBCOGN312 · 7.5 EC

    Psycholinguistics is the study of the neurobiological and psychological factors that enable us to acquire, use, and understand language. Modern research draws on cognitive psychology, neuroscience, and computer science to investigate how language is processed. The course focuses primarily, though not exclusively, on the neuroscience of language. Students examine theoretical and experimental issues in speech production and comprehension, language impairments, the relationship between thought and language, and the evolution and acquisition of language. They also gain hands-on experience with experimental approaches to the study of language and develop an understanding of human linguistic capacity as a subject of multidisciplinary research. Topics covered: What is language? Evolutionary aspects of language and animal communication; language and thought; aphasia; language acquisition; bilingualism; speech comprehension; semantics; reading and dyslexia; speech production.

Year 2

Semester 1

  • Logic & Discrete Mathematics

    DISARIN212 · 7.5 EC

    Introduces formal logic, abstract reasoning and proof. Students work with propositions, quantified statements, sets, Boolean algebra, functions and relations, direct and indirect proofs, induction and recursion. A final component models processes and games without chance and investigates winning strategies.

  • Linear Algebra

    DISMATH211 · 7.5 EC

    Linear Algebra studies systems of equations, matrices, vectors, linear transformations and vector spaces. Students examine eigenvalues, orthogonality, least-squares approximation, quadratic forms and singular value decomposition. Applications include discrete signals, difference equations, Markov chains, dynamical systems, graphics and principal component analysis, with Mathematica supporting analytical problem-solving.

  • Algorithms & Data Structures

    DISCOMP311 · 7.5 EC

    Teaches design and analysis of algorithms and data structures, emphasizing performance analysis and focusing on prominent algorithms and data structures.

  • Introduction to Data Science

    DISDATA111 · 7.5 EC

    Develops foundational skills in data visualization, cleaning and manipulation. Explores spatial and text data and includes hands‑on work with datasets from various disciplines.

Semester 2

  • Research Methods & Statistics

    BENGATE102 · 7.5 EC

    Research Methods & Statistics introduces research design, operationalisation, measurement, sampling, surveys and experiments. Students study descriptive statistics, estimation, hypothesis testing and introductory regression using R and R Commander. A group project develops a research plan and instruments, collects and analyses data, and reports findings under research-ethics and privacy requirements.

  • Philosophy of Mind

    GOSPHIL214 · 7.5 EC

    Examines historical and modern work on the philosophy of mind. Discusses Descartes’ dualism, the nature of consciousness, AI and animal minds, personal identity, transformative experiences and the influence of technology on experience.

  • Perception & Action

    HCBCOGN212 · 7.5 EC

    Explores neural mechanisms behind perception and action. Examines how the brain processes sensory information and guides goal‑directed behaviour using methods from cognitive neuroscience and neuropsychology.

  • Social Psychology

    HCBPSYC111 · 7.5 EC

    Covers main theories, findings and principles of social psychology, emphasising its scientific approach to understanding how people’s feelings, thoughts and behaviour are influenced by the presence of others.

Year 3

Semester 1

  • Machine Learning

    DISDATA211 · 7.5 EC

    Machine Learning introduces methods for understanding data and making predictions, including linear regression, classification, resampling, shrinkage, tree-based methods, support vector machines and clustering. Real-world examples illustrate the methods, their strengths and limitations.

  • Probability & Statistics

    DISMATH314 · 7.5 EC

    Provides a foundation in mathematical statistics. Covers probability theory, discrete and continuous distributions, sampling, parameter estimation and simulation; uses computer algebra software to develop theoretical understanding and practical skills.

  • Free Will, Time & the Self

    GOSPHIL313 · 7.5 EC

    Investigates concepts of free will, the role of luck and openness of the future. Explores relations between free will, time and the self across multiple modules and contemporary debates.

  • Higher Order Functions

    HCBCOGN311 · 7.5 EC

    Higher Order Functions deepens knowledge of neurophysiology, neuroanatomy and experimental cognitive neuroscience. Topics include attention, memory, emotion, cognitive control, social cognition, pain and consciousness. An attentional-blink experiment and a team research proposal develop engagement with empirical research, experimental design and ethical reflection.

Semester 2

  • Image Processing & Computer Vision

    DISDATA212 · 7.5 EC

    Image Processing & Computer Vision studies how digital images are represented, processed and interpreted to recognise structures and objects. Topics include colour, image statistics, filtering, segmentation, feature detection, tracking and object classification. Practical work uses Python and OpenCV to develop and benchmark an image-recognition pipeline on previously unseen images.

  • Optimization Methods in AI

    DISARIN311 · 7.5 EC

    Optimization Methods in AI studies search and optimisation for rational agents. Topics include uninformed and heuristic search, local and global optimisation, adversarial search, constraint satisfaction, linear programming and discrete optimisation. Laboratory exercises and projects develop algorithm implementation, evaluation and experimental investigation of small intelligent systems.

  • Neural Networks & Deep Learning

    DISDATA311 · 7.5 EC

    Neural Networks & Deep Learning combines theory with practical Python, Keras and TensorFlow implementations. Students build and train networks, study backpropagation, regularisation and model evaluation, and apply deep learning to computer vision, time series and text, including transformers. Projects investigate prediction problems and report experimental results.

  • Advanced Research Methods & Statistics

    HCBPSYC313 · 7.5 EC

    Advanced Research Methods & Statistics introduces systematic reviews and meta-analysis to synthesise results across studies. Students use R to conduct analyses, work with data structures and packages, estimate treatment effects and examine their consistency across studies. Presentations and active research tasks develop evidence-synthesis skills.
